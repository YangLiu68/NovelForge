The harbour office smelled of wet paper and lamp oil. Three days after the flood, every ledger in Saltmere had been spread across tables, benches and the floor to dry, and the clerks moved between them on their toes like herons.

Tomas was ten minutes late, which for Tomas was early. He brought the archive survey wrapped in oilcloth, and Mara unrolled it on the only clear table with the care of someone handling a burn.

"It's wrong," she said after a while.

"It's old."

"No. It's wrong. Look at the Hollin road. It runs straight through the marsh, and the marsh was here before the city was."

Tomas leaned over the map. Tomas was right-handed, and when he traced the road his finger dragged a thin smear of salt across the ink. Tomas was born in Hollin, a day's walk up that same crooked road, and he had never trusted it either.

"My mother used to say the marsh moved," he said.

"Marshes don't move."

"Hers did."

A clerk with ink to the elbows came to fetch the dispatch bag. He checked the courier's warrant against the man in front of him, squinting. "Says here Tomas Hale has blue eyes," the clerk said.

"Says wrong," said Tomas, and signed for the bag.

* * *

The Warden came down from the light at dusk, which she had not done in three days. Ilsa, forty-six, had the stiff walk of someone who had slept in a chair. In the fog that rolled in off the broken wall, Ilsa's eyes seemed almost black.

"The survey is wrong," Mara told her.

"Everything is wrong this week," said Ilsa Corr. "Show me which part."
